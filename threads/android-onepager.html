What your Android remembers ▍

Less than you'd fear, because modern Android encrypts storage behind your lock screen. Here's what it keeps and how to clear each by hand.

github.com/anthonyonazure/privacy-sweep — full writeup + laptop scripts
01Why there's no cleanup app
Every app is walled off from every other, and storage is encrypted with a key tied to your lock screen. No normal app can reach another's data or the protected databases.
02Deleted photos
They sit in Trash up to 60 days. Google Photos › Library › Trash › empty.
03Browser history, cookies, cache
Chrome › ⋮ › Delete browsing data › Time range: All time › tick all › Clear
04Per-app caches
Apps stockpile thumbnails and downloads.
Settings › Apps › (app) › Storage & cache › Clear cache
05Location & ad tracking
Maps › your photo › Your Timeline › settings › delete history. And Settings › Privacy › Ads › Delete advertising ID.
★The one move that clears everything

Settings › System › Reset options › Erase all data (factory reset). Because storage is encrypted, this throws away the key and makes the whole contents unrecoverable at once. A strong lock screen is what protects it all day to day.

Scope: personal privacy hygiene on your own device. Not a list of everywhere a forensic examiner looks, and not a way to defeat an investigation.
