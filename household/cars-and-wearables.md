# Cars, watches, and trackers

## Your car

Modern cars send driving data to the maker, and some makers have sold it.

- **GM and OnStar owners:** under a final federal order, GM must get consent, honor a
  request for a copy and for deletion, and let you opt out of sharing.
- **Data brokers that hold driving records:** LexisNexis has a consumer form to request
  your disclosure report, opt out, and delete (consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request, or
  1-888-497-0011). Verisk takes driving behavior requests by mailed form and needs your
  VIN. It says its data comes only from GM, Hyundai, and Honda.
- **Car app owner list:** open the car's phone app and review who is an authorized user.
  Someone added there keeps control of the car through the app.
- **Selling or returning a car:** factory reset the infotainment system, disconnect your
  phone, remove the car from its phone app, and cancel subscriptions. A reset does not
  close the account with the maker, so close or unlink that too.

## Watches and trackers

| Item | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Apple Watch | Unpair from the iPhone Watch app. Erasing on the watch alone leaves Activation Lock on. |
| AirTag | Find My, then Remove Item. |
| Galaxy Watch | Factory reset, then unpair. |
| Fitbit | Google account sign-in replaced the old login in May 2026, and deletion of unmigrated data began in July 2026. Deleting the account does not erase the device. Factory reset the device too. |

Not covered (check first): Garmin, Oura, Tile, and retention periods for car-maker apps.

## Someone else's tracker on you

- **Android:** Settings, then Safety and emergency, has unknown-tracker alerts.
- **iPhone:** act on the "Item Detected Near You" alert.
- Turning Bluetooth off does not stop a tracker someone else placed with you.
