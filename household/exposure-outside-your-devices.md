# What others hold about your home

Some of what is known about your home was never on your devices. Cars with cameras and
phones passing by collect it. These steps ask the companies to stop showing or keeping it.

## Your Wi-Fi network on location maps

Phones and cars record the name and hardware address (BSSID) of nearby Wi-Fi, and location
databases use that to place devices indoors.

- **Add `_nomap` to the end of the network name** (for example `HomeNet_nomap`). Google
  honors it. Apple is reported to honor it (check first). Others are unconfirmed.
- **Renaming does not erase the past.** The record is keyed to the router's BSSID, not the
  name. To remove an existing entry you have to ask each database.
- **WiGLE** is a public map of Wi-Fi networks collected by volunteers. To have your network
  removed, email its administrators and include your router's BSSID. Success depends on them
  (check first). The BSSID is printed on the router's label and shown in its admin page.
- **Do this on a network you own.** Renaming forces every device to rejoin.

## Street-level photos of your house

| Service | How to ask | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Street View | Open your address in Street View, choose Report a problem, then Request blurring, then My home. | A blur is reported to be permanent. Assume you cannot undo it later, so decide first. |
| Apple Look Around | Email MapsImageCollection@apple.com with your address and what to blur. | Reply times vary. |
| Bing Streetside | Reporting steps could not be confirmed (check first). | Treat blurring as unreliable. |

A blur hides the image only. Your address stays in the property and mapping records
(see [accounts.md](accounts.md) for the people-search sites that list it).

## What this does not cover

- **Satellite and aerial imagery.** No general blur-request route was confirmed.
- **Drone no-fly registries.** Lists exist, but they are voluntary and not enforced, so they
  do not keep a drone out.
